Why Proper Waste Disposal Matters

IHazardous waste isn't just paint, used gasoline, or oil—batteries, light bulbs, and electronics are dangerous, too. There are not enough measures to prevent most from tossing them in the garbage. When this happens, however, there are problems for our health and the environment. When batteries are thrown in the trash, cadmium, lead, mercury, and other heavy metals seep into the Earth. According to the Environmental Protection Agency, these heavy metals pollute soil and groundwater, which makes soil uninhabitable and unfarmable. Furthermore, when people throw away bulbs, they release mercury vapor into the air. According to the Agency for Toxic Substances and Disease Registry, this pollutes mercury in water sources and subsequently, bioaccumulation in marine life. People living close to informal dump sites are at a higher risk of developing respiratory disease, neuropathy, and specific forms of cancer due to the highly toxic exposure and waste. Furthermore, exposure to metals in old wires and machinery can cause lead poisoning. This poisoning has been shown to decrease children’s IQ. Recycling is a much better way to get rid of these toxic materials. According to the International Solid Waste Association, recovery of such metals generates billions yearly. Unfortunately, most of these metals end up in landfills and are not properly recycled. Ultimately, the answer will be education, policy, and technology, such as awareness efforts and governmental policy measures.
